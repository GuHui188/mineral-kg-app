## S12
来源：津巴布韦《基础矿产出口管制（未选冶含锂矿石）令》（Statutory Instrument 213 of 2022, Base Minerals Export Control (Unbeneficiated Lithium Bearing Ores) Order）
发布单位：津巴布韦矿业与矿业发展部；发布时间：2022年12月
转引自：路透社、津巴布韦矿业部声明及行业分析（TSM Hub、Everstream Analytics）

In December 2022, Zimbabwe banned exports of raw, unprocessed lithium ore under Statutory Instrument 213 of 2022 — the Base Minerals Export Control (Unbeneficiated Lithium Bearing Ores) Order — requiring written ministerial permission for any exception. The ban requires companies to process the mineral before shipment, in a bid to encourage domestic investment and create jobs.

The policy followed Indonesia's successful export ban on nickel ore, and quickly became an industrial-policy tool: it attracted significant Chinese investment into local lithium processing, including a $300 million investment from Chengxin Lithium and a refinery by Zhejiang Huayou Cobalt — Africa's first lithium sulphate processing facility. Lithium revenue jumped from $70 million in 2022 to more than $600 million in 2024 as the ban pushed Chinese buyers to build concentrators on Zimbabwean soil. Prospect Lithium Zimbabwe, owned by China's Zhejiang Huayou Cobalt, saw its lithium carbonate plant reach about 90 percent completion by mid-2026.

Zimbabwe is Africa's leading lithium producer and a key supplier to China, accounting for approximately 15% of China's total lithium import volumes in 2025. The country shipped 1.128 million tonnes of spodumene concentrate in 2025, an 11% increase on 2024, with the overwhelming majority destined for Chinese conversion plants.

The policy has since tightened further: on 10 June 2025, the government announced that exports of lithium concentrates would also be prohibited from January 2027, requiring producers to convert concentrate into lithium sulphate — a higher-value chemical intermediate used to make battery-grade lithium carbonate or hydroxide. On 25 February 2026, the Ministry of Mines suspended all raw-mineral and lithium-concentrate exports with immediate effect, citing "irregularities and losses", later softening the freeze into an export tax plus a producer-specific quota system tied to written commitments on sulphate plant timelines.
