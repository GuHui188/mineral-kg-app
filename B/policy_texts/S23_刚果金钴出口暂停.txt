## S23
来源：刚果（金）战略矿产市场监管与控制局（ARECOMS）2025年第001号决定（钴出口暂停令）
转引自：国际能源署 IEA 政策数据库（iea.org/policies/28969）及路透社报道
事件时间：2025年2月22日发布；2025年10月15日由出口配额制取代

On February 22, 2025, the Authority for the Regulation and Control of Strategic Mineral Substances' Markets (ARECOMS) imposed Decision No. 001/ARECOMS/2025 of February 22, 2025, suspending the export of cobalt from the Democratic Republic of Congo and Communication No. 2025/003. The suspension is applicable to all types of cobalt mining, including industrial, semi-industrial and artisanal.

This was a temporary suspension on cobalt exports to be in force until 21 June 2025, in response to the high level of stock on the market. ARECOMS published a new communication extending the ban for three additional months until 21 September 2025. On 21 September 2025, ARECOMS extended the ban further to 15 October 2025, along with a new policy establishing an export quota, to be implemented from 16 October 2025.

Under the new quota framework, allocations for the fourth quarter of 2025 are based on producers' export volumes between 2022 and 2024. Miners were allowed to ship just over 18,000 tonnes of cobalt during the remainder of 2025, and up to 96,600 tonnes per year in 2026 and 2027 — volumes amounting to less than half of national output in 2024. ARECOMS warned that export quotas will be revoked from companies that fail to meet volume targets, breach environmental or tax rules, or transfer allocations to third parties. Firms must prepay mining royalties according to monthly quotas before loading shipments.

The Democratic Republic of Congo accounts for about three quarters of global cobalt supply. The suspension, imposed after cobalt prices fell to a record low amid oversupply, sought to stabilise the market and encourage domestic processing. Chinese mining giant CMOC, the world's largest cobalt producer with two mines in the DRC (Tenke-Fungurume and Kisanfu), has been among the most affected; its trading arm IXM declared force majeure on cobalt delivery contracts. Glencore, the second-largest cobalt producer, stockpiled its DRC cobalt in the country and declared force majeure for some deliveries. Cobalt prices more than doubled since the suspension; cobalt hydroxide, the main product exported from the DRC, rose roughly fourfold.
